## Progression
### Overview

*Arcanum*'s progression is unique in how it's one of the only systems in the original that doesn't fall apart completely during regular gameplay. While there are definitely some imbalances and a bundle of confusing elements (which we will discuss at length later in this section), during an average playthrough, it still generally succeeds in its goal of keeping players engaged, making them feel like their actions and choices have impact, and providing a genuinely fun and engaging system that keeps the game working from start to finish.

It might be messy, it might be confusing, it might even be objectively bad from a design perspective, but unlike so much else in the original, it *works*, and that core experience should be preserved in a remake.

Taking from more modern *RPG*s like *Pillars of Eternity*, *Divinity: Original Sin*, and *Baldur's Gate 3*, *ARCANUM REMADE* will rebuild its progression to fix the main problems with the original, an overabundance of choice with minimal mechanical difference, while aiming to keep everything that made it recognizably *Arcanum*.

This means lots and *lots* of content being cut and truncated, entire categories and classes being grouped together, and even a fair amount of entirely *new* content designed specifically to counter one of the original's biggest failings: the monstrous imbalance between content for tech and magick users.

To start, lets work our way from the basics and take a look at the foundation of the stat system:
### Attributes

In its original form, Arcanum is somewhat infamous for its strange and pointless stat spread. Attributes like "beauty" and "charisma" overlap in unclear ways and are functionally useless to the point that you can easily experience all of the content the game has to offer without so much as considering them during leveling.

In *ARCANUM REMADE*, the core attribute pool has been consolidated and more clearly delineated to make character builds simpler, more intuitive, and mechanically distinct. The main attributes, now **six** are:

1. Strength
	Carrying capacity and physical damage output, heavy arms affinity
2. Constitution
	Health, damage-resistance
3. Dexterity
	Speed, action points in combat
4. Intelligence
	Crafting and general wits, bartering and persuasion
5. Willpower
	The strength of will required to bend the laws of nature (as per *Arcanum*'s lore), affects spell ability strength and magick attack resistances
6. Perception
	Precision and stealth, light and explosive arms affinity

These attributes will all give a quantifiable bonus to certain actions and statistics, communicated clearly to the player in the level-up interface; no more cryptic level 20 bonuses and unclear affinities.

Attributes are determined based on the character's starting race and background, giving them a head start on a certain class or build. Throughout the game, attributes may be enhanced on level-up, with each level granting characters **three** assignable attribute points.

In addition to granting simple stat affinities, attributes are also directly tied to access to class abilities and their efficacy, dividing classes into mechanically unique, but familiar archetypes that players can easily work into their working understanding of genre conventions.

In addition to attributes though, players will also choose from a set of:
### Skills

Skills represent unique bonuses with benefits and drawbacks. As opposed to attributes, they don't have a numerical level and are entirely unique, similar to *Fallout*'s "perks" or *Divinity: Original Sin*'s "talents".

While attributes are directly correlated with classes, skills are more free-form, existing to support certain playstyles and builds within classes, adding an additional layer of choice to character builds.

Certain skills are inherent based on a character's starting race or class, while others can be acquired at specific level intervals, making them more special and difficult to acquire than attributes.

This bears the question—what about *Arcanum*'s original 16 skills? To put it succinctly, they've been cut, their essence redistributed between the new skills and abilities. In their original form, skills were simply too trivial and uninteresting, providing minimal effect and being exceptionally easy to level within the first few *hours* of the game.

Far too much of *Arcanum*'s side content gives the player either no meaningful reward, or a minor stat bonus that isn't explained outside of the *190 page manual*. With the new skill system, players will be able to receive truly unique rewards that meaningfully impact their gameplay experience and actually mechanically encourage them to engage with content that isn't strictly necessary for progression of the main quest.
### Abilities

One of the most egregious failings of the original *Arcanum* has to be the way that abilities are handled. Namely, despite being a game nominally about magick *and* tech, *Arcanum* seems to go out of its way to skew their balance as much as possible. Sorcerers are given a frankly comical amount of active abilities, letting them do almost anything, from opening locked chests to teleporting across the entire map. Meanwhile, technicians have... guns? And bombs too, of course; how could I forget?

This is simply unacceptable—an imbalance of this level completely breaks game balance and makes any reason to play as a technician besides roleplay *nonexistent*. As such, one of the main goals of the new ability system is to provide an equal amount of variety and content between magick and tech, strengthening the game's narrative core while also just making it more engaging and fun.

Everything that was previously categorized as a "spell" as part of the "spell college" system will be adapted into this category, with the colleges themselves gutted thoroughly and unrecognizably, only leaving the very best spells and concepts.

To understand the new divide better, now is perhaps a great time to take a closer look at the new and improved...
### Classes

Perhaps one of the most striking design decisions in the original *Arcanum* is the lack of a clear, ironclad divide between classes and their abilities. Multi-classing is at least limited through the magick/tech affinity bar, but no effort is made to guide players within those boundaries. This, in effect, gives *Arcanum* only two real classes, sorcerer and technician, with no reason given to players not to master every single skill within their chosen area, especially with the baffling max level of *50*.

*ARCANUM REMADE* cuts down on this complexity while not undermining players' free choice by:
- still allowing free multi-classing (whilst balancing individual classes with the option in mind)
- giving more limited, but *clearer* and more *impactful* build choices
- offering unlimited *respec*s to let players experiment freely and find their preferred playstyle at their own pace 

More concretely, there are ***eight*** defined classes, four tech and four magick, all serving as counterparts, supports, and foils to each other.

Starting from the tech side, we have:
#### Gunslinger

The basic tech class, most similar to the current tech playstyle in *Arcanum*. Gunslingers specialize in light arms (revolvers, rifles, and shotguns). Excelling in *Dexterity* and *Perception*, they serve as the quickest and most versatile and quick damage dealers in combat, also possessing practical skills like thievery and lock-picking.
#### Cannoneer

Specializing in extremely heavy arms, like flamethrowers or full-blown cannons, Cannoneers combine *Strength* and *Constitution* to make the archetypal "tank" of the tech classes, taking physical damage well and even dealing formidable single-target damage themselves.
#### Demolitions Expert

*Intelligence* and *Perception*, crafting and handling experimental and patchwork weapons is their specialty. They're capable of supporting their party while offering area denial through traps and area-of-effect weapons.
#### Wind-up Mechanist

The odd one out, the only completely original class that has no counterpart in the original; the mechanist serves as the summoner/support of the tech family of classes. Investing in *Intelligence* and *Dexterity*, these technicians can build and direct constructs along with helpful support items.

Moving on to the magick classes, we start with the simple...
#### Elementalist

The most basic of all sorcerers, Elementalists are meant to be the most accessible and widely applicable magick class. Specializing in *Willpower* and *Strength*, these mages inherit from all of the elemental colleges from the original game, further refined for more variety.
#### Necromancer

*Arcanum*'s most specialest and cherished class; Necromancers represent the core of *Arcanum*'s main story and many of its most influential characters.

To preserve the uniqueness of the original, the new Necromancer class will preserve both aspects of the original, Necromantic Black and White, while lumping in aspects of Nature and Summoning to create a dual summoner/support class, preserving the core of the original while creating a direct counterpart to the new Mechanist class.

Levelling *Willpower* and *Dexterity*, Necromancers will balance controlling minions to fight for them and supporting their allies with status buffs and healing.
#### Space-Time Dominator

Perhaps the most exotic, and certainly the most self-indulgent. Their lofty title has them equipped with the more abstract and conceptual colleges: Time, Conveyance, Divination, and Meta.

Combining *Willpower* with *Intelligence*, these mages are crafty, using all sorts of items in combination with their strange abilities to both support allies and take out enemies themselves.
#### Force Mage

The "tank" of the magick classes, Force Mages shield their allies and attack with direct application of force, combining the Force, Morph, and Phantasm colleges. With *Willpower* and *Constitution*, they stand as a key role in the front lines of battle in any magick-heavy party.

Now that we have a simple understanding of levelling and classes, let's take a look at how it's split and paced throughout the game—
### Pacing Levelling

In order to better balance its classes and pace progression more evenly, *ARCANUM REMADE* will impose a level limit of *20*, over half that of the original. This smaller limit lets content be spaced more dense and forces builds to be more narrow and focused.

Character points, *Arcanum*'s universal upgrade resource has also being scrapped entirely; its implementation and design philosophy simply being too unfocused for the new system. Instead, Attributes, Skills, and Abilities are all received separately at fixed level intervals.

Finally, with the reimagining of the combat system and subsequent removal of random combat encounters during exploration, experience points have been more equally distributed between combat and quests, as per *Arcanum*'s intended design vision.

Quest rewards in *ARCANUM REMADE* won't just be limited to items and experience points though, they will also include a completely new resource that changes the dynamics of progression and combat:
### Fate Actions

As is customary for anything original in Arcanum, Fate Points are a genuinely interesting and fun idea that, in its original form, is sadly held back by a profound lack of *clarity* on what they do and how they can be obtained, making them so rare that hoarding them is only natural, further making the player disappointed when they discover that they have little to no *impact* in actual gameplay.

Conceptually though, Fate Points are a fascinating idea and one of the few pure mechanics that are truly unlike anything else in the genre. For the purposes of *ARCANUM REMADE*, Fate Points will be reimagined from the ground up as Fate *Actions* to provide a *clear* and *profound* impact during gameplay.

To keep them relatively balanced though, Fate Actions will adhere to a simple maxim:

**EQUIVALENT EXCHANGE**

To explain, let's try to examine what exactly Fate Points are supposed to be: a *deus ex machina*, a single-use divine intervention, something that by its very nature *breaks* systems and causes unexpected results. Put like this, we see how Fate points are an inherently dangerous system to balance, something that can easily get out of hand if not handled correctly.

To preempt this catastrophe a few general rules will be laid out here:
- Any and all Fate Actions must have a corresponding "sink", a scenario designed expressly as an output for them
- Acquisition of Fate Actions must be limited by player strength and the wider progression system
- All Fate Actions, without exception, are *single-use*

With all of that preamble, you might be wondering—"Oh writer dearest, what might these "Fate Actions" actually *do*?" That's the tricky part; but we'll untangle it bit by bit by asking the more concrete question of...
#### How do Fate Actions actually look mechanically?

Now this is much more simple! To put it succinctly, Fate Actions are can represent any kind single-use action that can be taken during combat while not being beholden to the rules and restrictions of combat. Their exact content varies depending on the exact nature and difficulty of the quest they were received from, but they generally provide powerful bonuses that can change the course of a battle.

Imagine anything from temporary stat bonuses to instant revives and full party heals, there's really no necessary limit to their effects as long as we keep our design maxims in mind. As to their acquisition, that leads us into our next section:
#### How are Fate Actions acquired?

Through quests, depending on the quest's difficulty. Every single quest in *ARCANUM REMADE* will give exactly one Fate Action, its exact effect being unique to that specific quest, relating to its difficulty and scaling  in strength proportionally.

The goal is to have players gathering Fate Actions throughout a playthrough, then giving them a sense of meaning and accomplishment when they use them during a particularly rough encounter, openly rewarding them for doing the required additional content.
#### TLDR?

Fate Actions are a new, unique, single-use combat resource that allows players to drastically alter battles, acquired from completing quests.
